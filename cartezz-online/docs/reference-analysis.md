# Reference analysis: Cartezz // @murthered

The house style of the procedural-film skill is cream paper cut against navy blueprint.
This film asks for a different look — a dark, late-night internet aesthetic — so, as the skill requires, the look is analysed first and art-bible sections 1–9 are rewritten from the rules at the end of this file.

## Sources

1. **The director's written brief** (the request that started this project): the full shot list, the visual identity list, directing, audio and technical notes, plus one addition sent later: *somewhere, Cartezz looks at his own profile, surprised and admiring*.
2. **Cartezz's profile screenshot** (`.tmp/research/profile.png`, 1206 × 2622, a Telegram profile in dark mode). Colours below were sampled from its pixels. The avatar photo itself is not reproduced; it is redrawn as a stylised procedural portrait.

## What the brief fixes

| Item | Brief says | Read as a drawing rule |
|---|---|---|
| Environment | black and very dark gray | Base is near-black (#040306), never pure flat black: a faint vertical lift and haze give depth. |
| Accent | glowing purple accents | Purple is a light source and a trim colour, not a fill. At most ~12% of any frame's pixels read as purple. |
| Motifs | purple bow-tie / ribbon, purple cap | Two hero glyphs with one canonical drawing each (kit), reused at every scale: badge, sticker, sky-sized object, the character's own costume. |
| Avatar | mysterious profile/avatar feeling | The avatar is a dark stylised portrait (long dark hair, cap, face half in shadow, a dark city behind). At the end we learn it is the frozen final frame of the film: the whole city was inside it. |
| UI | small bright UI-like elements floating in darkness | Tiny rounded bars, dots and pills in pale lilac-white at low alpha; the "online" dot is the brightest small thing in any frame. |
| Tone | surreal but coherent, cinematic not childish | One consistent world with real perspective, fog, rim light and restrained motion. No bouncing, no cartoon squash. |
| Humour | from the absurd situation, not from the character | Cartezz is always composed and dignified. The camera treats every absurd object as monumental. |

## Shot grammar taken from the brief

- **Scale arc:** tiny UI → UI multiplies → UI becomes a city → the city fills with absurd giant objects → one button → freeze → the city was inside the avatar. The film is one continuous nested space, so the geometry of the profile card is shared by the first and last shots.
- **Prestige sci-fi camera:** symmetric one-point street perspective, slow dollies, low angles, silhouettes against light, a slow push on the face at the climax, one rapid pull-back as payoff.
- **Tension then escalation:** the first ~20 s add one element at a time; absurdity escalates only in the walk.
- **The freeze:** every moving thing — objects, lights, particles, and even the film grain — stops on the press frame.

## Style rules to carry over

1. **Two plates, both dark.** *Screen plate*: the flat phone interface, orthographic, hairline UI on near-black. *City plate*: the same UI language built as a 3D world — perspective, fog, metallic dark faces, purple rim light. Plates alternate in the notification act and the reveal; the walk lives on the city plate with inserts.
2. **Darkness first.** Every frame is at least 75% near-black. Light comes from few sources: the online dot, notification glows, neon trim, the ONLINE button.
3. **Purple is an accent.** Purple lines, glows and motif objects over black and dark gray. Never a purple background wash.
4. **UI language everywhere.** Rounded rectangles, pills, dots, list rows (dot + two bars), hairlines, corner brackets. Buildings are made of these: facades are feeds of list rows, towers are stacked cards.
5. **Motifs with a job.** Bow tie and caps = gifts (attention from the outside world), and also Cartezz's own costume; tamagotchi = the strange digital gift; notification pill = the outside world calling; online dot / `2 online` = presence. Each appears first small in the UI, then physical in the city, then frozen.
6. **One character drawing.** Cartezz — a young man in a long black coat, purple cap, indigo bow tie with a gold star, dark medium-length hair, pale face half in shadow — is drawn by one shared kit function in every shot. (The director confirmed: Cartezz is a guy.)
7. **Motion.** Camera moves at full 24 fps and eases slowly; objects drift, spin and bob slowly; UI pops are crisp (outBack, 3 frames); the character walks on twos. Nothing is floaty or bouncy.
8. **Hard cuts on the beat**, with two exceptions owned by the story: the world-break and the pull-back.
9. **Grain** is the fine dark "schematic" grain of the engine, boiling at 12 fps — and frozen from the press onward.
10. **Text is UI.** Only interface words appear: `12:14`, `Edit`, `cartezz`, `2 online`, `call` `unmute` `search` `more`, `twerk - cartezz`, `username`, `@murthered`, gift numbers, notification counts, `ONLINE`, and the end title `CARTEZZ // @MURTHERED`. No dialogue.
11. **The four gifts are the only objects.** Bow tie (indigo, gold star), purple cap, white cap (paper-plane logo), 2025 tamagotchi. No generic gift boxes.
12. **The awe beat.** Mid-walk, a giant hologram of his own profile hangs over the street; he stops and looks up at it, surprised and admiring. Dignified: widened eyes, raised brows, a small open-mouthed smile, lit by his own profile.
